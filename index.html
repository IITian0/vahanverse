How to read this

EVs per public charger is the number that matters: how many electric vehicles share each public charging point. A low number means charging is easy to find; a high number means drivers queue.

Each state is coloured by that number, in four bands:

Adequate — 20 or fewer EVs per charger
Watch — 21 to 32
Underserved — 33 to 48
Critical — more than 48
What the slider does

The slider lets you add new public chargers and watch the map re-colour. It sends them to the states that need them most, so you can see exactly how many chargers it takes to lift a state out of Underserved.

The panel on the right of the map shows the priority order — which states to fix first.

Numbers are illustrative and shown to demonstrate the method; the live platform is populated from VAHAN registrations and public charger inventories.

In plain words

In Bihar, about 57 EVs share each public charger — nearly twice the national average of 29.3. That is the clearest gap in the country.

The green states are the metro cores, where charging has kept up. The amber and red states are where adoption has arrived ahead of the chargers.

How it works

From scattered sources to one grid.

Charger lists live in a dozen places; registrations live in VAHAN. VahanVerse brings them onto one comparable grid.

01

Ingest & standardise

One schema for every source.

02

Geocode & validate

Checked against boundaries.

03

De-duplicate

One charger listed twice becomes one.

04

Aggregate to grid

Rolled up to H3 cells.

05

Map RTO to geography

Registrations land on the same cells.

The platform

Four layers, one national view.

01

Harmonisation

Ingest, clean and de-duplicate every source, then project it onto one H3 hexagon grid.

02

Analytics

The Charging Adequacy Index, gap classification and demand models for every cell.

03

Decision

Rank regions by priority and simulate where the next chargers should go.

04

Interface

A national map and a planner dashboard anyone can read and act on.
